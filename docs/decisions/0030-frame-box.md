# 0030 — The Frame's box: the Position is the one source of the Frame's shape

**Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Layer:** system (contracts, Central's Frames, Displays and Planner, the Player, Display Host's EDID report, the console) · **Status:** accepted design, built on the branch `feat/frame-box-position` (one pull request), after [0029](0029-render-planes.md) (v0.41.0). **Decision owner:** the owner, in chat. His words below are his requirements; everything not quoted from him is a design choice, not his ruling.

**Supersedes:** [0029](0029-render-planes.md)'s design rule 2 as it stood (a Frame is an upright rectangle of its profile's aspect: centre and width, the height from the profile) and the Frame display profile (`width_px`, `height_px`, `diagonal_inches`, `video`) with the wall placement's width and height. **Builds on:** [0029](0029-render-planes.md) (planes, the turn, the finish size), [0028](0028-room-light.md) (the room's light, which needs the Frame's size in millimetres), [0025](0025-configuration-snapshots.md) (Position history), [0023](0023-print-look.md) (the mat), which keep their rules.

**The breakdown he agreed (R3):** (1) the Position (left, top, width and height of the Frame's box on the Display's screen as it hangs, plus the screen's turn) is the one source of the Frame's shape and pixel size, and photos are judged and prepared from it; (2) the wall placement keeps only where the Frame hangs on the wall, its physical size coming from the Display's real size scaled by the box, not typed again; (3) the profile shrinks to what is not geometry (whether video is allowed) or folds into the Frame's own settings, and `diagonal_inches` goes, its users taking the physical size from the Display's real size.

## Decision

- **The Position is a box** (`contracts/models.py` `PositionBox`: `left`, `top`, `width`, `height`, each a fraction of the screen as it hangs; `contracts/frame_fit.py` `FrameBox`). Width and height are set independently (R1); the Frame's shape is whatever the box is. Design rule 2 of [0029](0029-render-planes.md) now reads: a Frame is an upright box on its screen as it hangs.
- **Fractions, not pixels.** A box in fractions survives a mode change (1080p to 4K keeps the box; the pixels follow) and gives the physical size without the mode. `place_box` turns a box into whole pixels, each edge rounded half up, so two boxes sharing an edge share its pixel column. Cost: on a mode of another aspect than the panel (1280 x 1024 on a 16:9 panel) the box's pixel shape changes with it; the Frame output follows, so photos are judged by what is drawn.
- **A box always lies on its screen.** `FrameBox` refuses `left + width > 1` or `top + height > 1` at construction, and `place_box` places every box on every screen at least 1 x 1. The off-screen refusal (`position_off_screen`), `off_screen` and the Player's failed placement are gone: a guarantee moved from a runtime check to construction.
- **The Frame's pixels, shape and physical size are worked out, never typed.** Pixels: `place_box` of the box on the screen's mode (`contracts/models.py` `frame_output`, `frame_placement`). Shape: those pixels' ratio (`Placement.aspect`). Physical size: the box's share of the Display's picture area (`frame_size_mm`, `box_size_mm`).
- **The screen's size has one rule** (`central/displays/model.py` `screen_px`): the Output's mode the Pi reported when at least 1 x 1, else the preferred mode of the Display seen there (Weston runs the preferred mode when given none). Cost: a Pi whose Weston runs another mode than both is judged at the wrong size (a gap from before).
- **No known screen, no shape, no photos.** The profile was the fallback shape; there is none now. `central/wall_plan/fit.py` `fit_target` answers None, the Frame's slots take no photo (the empty-pool rule: keep the last still, else the fallback), no copy is sized for it, the fit analysis lists it under `unsized`, authoring a photo onto it is refused (`authored_frame_size_unknown`) and a Source preview for it answers `frame_size_unknown`. Cost: such a Frame shows nothing, but it could not show anything anyway.
- **The Display's size is its EDID base block's** (bytes 21 and 22, whole centimetres, times 10 mm; `appliance/display_host/edid.py`); detailed timing descriptors' millimetre sizes are never read. Evidence (2026-10-19, read on the production Pi): the Samsung QN43Q8F states 95 x 54 cm in its base block (43 inches across: right) and 1872 x 1053 mm in its preferred timing (85 inches: wrong). Either byte 0 reads as no size. Display Host reports it on each Output report (`OutputReport.size_mm`, the display line's schema 10); Central keeps the latest stated size on the Display (`displays.width_mm`, `height_mm`; a report with none keeps the last known), shows it on the Hardware tab and sends it on each binding (`OutputBinding.display_mm`).
- **No size stated: washes only, large for video, a dashed outline on the wall plan.** The Player draws only wash lights on that Frame (far and close lights and the mat shadow need millimetres); the large-Frame video rule (`large_frame`) counts it as large, so R4 holds; the wall plan draws it as a dashed 500 x 300 mm outline marked "size not known yet". Not taken: typing a Display's size on its Hardware tab (R3: "not typed again"); it is the first thing to add if a display he owns states none, one field on the Display, never on the Frame.
- **The large-Frame setting stays in inches** (`large_frame_inches`, "From this diagonal up"), compared with the diagonal worked out from the Frame's physical size. It is a threshold he reads on the settings page, not a stored copy of a Frame's shape.
- **"Shows videos" is the Frame's own setting** (`frames.video`, `PUT /v1/operator/frames/{frame_id}/video`, a switch on the Hardware tab): last write wins, no generation, configuration revision or Position check (it changes planning, not the picture). It is what was left of the profile (R3 point 3).
- **The wall placement keeps `surface_id`, `x_mm` and `y_mm`** (the Frame's top-left on the wall); the wall plan draws each Frame at its worked-out physical size. The orientation-coherence rule (`oriented_profile`) had nothing left to compare and is gone.
- **The binding carries the Display's size, not the Frame's** (`OutputBinding.display_mm`): a Position trial changes the box on the Pi, so the Player works the Frame's size out from the drawn box and the screen's turn with the same `frame_size_mm` Central uses. The calibration board takes its aspect from the placement's pixels.
- **Position history.** An entry's box is its own shape, so Restore no longer refuses an entry made for another profile (`made_for_other_profile` and the entry's profile columns are gone); it still refuses an entry made for another turn or Display. Stored `kept-profile-changed` entries stay readable; nothing writes the kind again.
- **Every stored copy migrates (as 075, 101 and 102)**: 103 adds the Display's size; 104 converts every stored Position (Frames, Position history, both trial copies, Players' last configurations, plan offers) to a box; 105 adds `frames.video` from the profile's; 106 moves stored bindings to `display_mm`; 107 drops the history's profile columns; 108 drops `frames.profile`, `width_mm` and `height_mm`. Forward-only, no CHECK, trigger or function.
- **The conversion is exact where the screen is known.** A rectangle becomes the box of its own pixels, which `place_box` places on exactly the same pixels, so a Frame whose screen is known keeps its Frame output, its photos and its readiness. A rectangle not wholly on its screen is clipped onto it, and a Frame with no known screen gets the whole screen; both are to check. Every Frame's calibration and configuration revisions rise by one, so every Player is sent its box and a draft made before the deploy is refused as stale. Not taken for an unknown screen: keeping the width share with the height from the profile's aspect (it guesses a shape R1 calls wrong).

## What it gives up, costs and defers

- **Gives up:** typing a Frame's size or resolution anywhere; a Frame's shape on a Pi that reports no mode and a Display that lists none (it gets no photos); room lamps, window light and the mat shadow on a display stating no size.
- **Costs:** the console keeps its own copy of `place_box` (`central/console/src/placement.js` `placeBox`), held to Central's pixel for pixel by a parity test (`tests/test_console_placement.py`): a convention, kept because a drag asks no round trip; nothing on Central needs to check a box. Migrations 102 and 104 restate the placement and the screen choice in SQL, tested against the Python. The display line's schema major and `OutputBinding` changed, so Central and the Pi move together on the release.
- **Deferred:** a typed Display size (above); the Player reporting the mode Weston runs, so Central never sizes photos for a stale enrolment report.

## Deploying

- **Take a `pg_dump` of Central's database before deploying.** Migrations 104 to 108 rewrite every stored Position in place and drop the profile and the wall size; `git revert` cannot undo them. The way back is that dump ([runbook](../runbook.md#deploying-the-frame-box-release)).
- **Deploy Central, then apply the Pi release at once.** Between the two, a Pi on the old release and Central disagree on the display line's schema (10 carries the Display's size) and on the binding (it carries a box and `display_mm`, no profile), so the old Player refuses every plan Central sends and its screens keep what they show until it takes the release. Releases do not follow by themselves. Each Pi on the new release reports its Display's size; until then a Frame draws washes only and counts as large for video.

## Considered and not taken

- **Pixels as the Position** (left, top, width, height in pixels of the mode): no rounding rule, but a rewrite on every mode change and a second rule for millimetres.
- **Keep the profile as the Frame's typed shape and let the box follow it** (R1's 16:9 complaint is exactly that coupling).
- **The Frame's physical size typed on the wall plan** (R2, R3: "not typed again").
